Projects and tickets are the two built-in tags with records behind them. Their values are real projects and tickets, so they can carry a ticket key pattern, repositories, a title and a parent.

What a project has

  • a ticket key pattern, such as ^KD-\d+
  • its repositories
  • tags, such as client: acme, which its token entries inherit
  • optionally a token budget and a cadence

A client is a tag on projects, not a record of its own. Tag several projects client: acme and cost per client is a filter. To make client a level of the cost object, add it as a rung of the ladder.

Tickets

A ticket is any unit of work: a feature, a story, an epic or a bug. It has a key and, where known, a title, a description, a parent ticket and an assignee. Only the key is required.

  • Synced. Connect Jira Cloud, Azure DevOps or GitHub Issues. Tickets sync every 15 minutes, or when you ask.
  • By hand. For work no ticket system knows, create a ticket in the web app. It works the same as a synced one.

A ticket can sit inside another, such as a story inside an epic. Cost rolls up: an epic's cost is the sum of its stories, and a project's cost is the sum of its tickets. A ticket's title and description are what token forecasts compare.

The assignee

The person a ticket is assigned to. Being assignee costs nothing: the cost of a ticket sits with the cost center of each token entry on it.

Cost that belongs to no ticket

Project work that belongs to no single ticket, such as research or setup, is tagged kind: project overhead. Work that belongs to no project at all is kind: organization overhead.
